There is a moment in every AI-supported process when speed tips over. The draft is ready. It sounds good. But nobody is sure which context was bound. Whether it contains a price that has not been approved. Whether someone formally agreed — or merely did not object.

This is not a prompting problem. This is a governance problem. Unitera starts there.

## Why Unitera is the Main Development
Unitera is the core of my current work: not just an AI tool, but a governance layer between generated draft and accountable business commitment.

The central thesis: AI can accelerate drafting, but it does not automatically solve accountability, approval, scope control, or auditability. That is exactly where Unitera starts.

## The starting problem
In B2B processes, proposals, RFP responses, and change requests often emerge from many sources: CRM context, pricing models, delivery dependencies, legal conditions, security claims, and customer expectations.

With AI, the first draft becomes faster. But the risk shifts backward:

- Was the right opportunity or CRM context bound?
- Does the draft contain discounts, special pricing, or delivery promises?
- Are there security, compliance, or liability claims?
- Is it clear which version was reviewed?
- Is it clear who may give final approval?
- Is there a defined Commit action?
- Is there a complete Evidence Timeline afterward?

Without these points, an AI-Draft remains fast, but not reliable.

## The system idea
Unitera models the transition from draft to Commitment as a controlled path. The focus is not on more text generation, but on the question: when is a draft allowed to become a binding commitment?

The path consists of eight layers:

- Intake: capture proposal type, Commitment context, and involved roles.
- Context Binding: bind and version opportunity, document, and rules context.
- Governed Draft: generate an AI-Draft in the bound context.
- Policy Evaluation: visibly separate runtime-near Gates and illustrative Decision Layers.
- Multi-Role Review: let Finance, Legal, and Sales review in defined queues.
- Approval Console: document Approve, Conditional Approve, Return, or Reject.
- Traceable Commit: bind approved version, payload hash, and target state.
- Audit Evidence: bring Gate classification, reviews, Approval, and Commit together in one timeline.

## What this shows about my working method
Unitera shows my working method most strongly because business risk, AI use, Backend logic, and visual explainability come together.

I work in four movements:

- Read risks: recognize price, scope, claims, roles, and handovers as a control problem.
- Model boundaries: separate AI-Draft, Policy Gate, Human Review, and Commit from each other.
- Place Authority: do not leave critical source of truth in the surface, but anchor it in defined states, roles, and Gates.
- Make Evidence visible: do not just make decisions, but make them versioned, verifiable, and traceable.

If you are building a system where AI-Drafts are meant to bring speed — but pricing, scope promises, or approvals must no longer end loosely: this is the point where this working method makes a concrete difference.
